## Executive Summary

This report covers the IAM Policy Audit (Access Control) of the `AllowEverything` policy statement. The review found a critical access control failure: the statement grants `Action: "*"` on `Resource: "*"` in a production environment, with no resource boundaries, no MFA requirement, no request conditions, and no session limits. The combined risk level is **CRITICAL**, and the final verdict is **ESCALATE**. The proposed remediation narrows the statement to application-specific S3, DynamoDB, and CloudWatch Logs actions, but the reviewer identified three gaps that must be resolved before implementation: the MFA condition would block non-interactive workloads, the DynamoDB resource list omits the index ARN required for `Query`, and other attached or assumable policies have not been checked for paths that could recreate the removed IAM, KMS, and delete permissions. Key actions are to split the statement by principal type, correct the DynamoDB resource ARNs, verify escalation paths through `sts:AssumeRole` and `iam:PassRole`, and obtain human sign-off on the production blast radius.

## Access Control (IAM) Findings

Risk Level: **CRITICAL**

The policy grants unrestricted administrative access to all actions and resources in a production environment with no MFA, conditions, or session limits, which is a critical access posture failure.

### Overly Broad Permissions

❌ **CRITICAL**: Unrestricted wildcard administrative grant.
- **Principal**: `AllowEverything`
- **Current Scope**: `Action: "*"`, `Resource: "*"`
- **Issue**: Every AWS API action is permitted on every resource in the account, including IAM (user, role, and policy management), KMS (key use and administration), and data-deletion operations.
- **Impact**: A single compromised credential using this statement can alter identity controls, disable or misuse encryption keys, and delete production data. Effective privilege is equivalent to full account administrator.
- **Least-Privilege Target**: Replace with a `ProdAppAccess` statement limited to `s3:GetObject`, `s3:PutObject`, `s3:ListBucket`, `dynamodb:GetItem`, `dynamodb:PutItem`, `dynamodb:UpdateItem`, `dynamodb:Query`, `logs:CreateLogStream`, and `logs:PutLogEvents`.

### Missing Resource Boundaries

❌ **CRITICAL**: No ARN scoping on any resource.
- **Policy**: `AllowEverything`
- **Affected Resources**: All resources (`*`), including production databases, KMS encryption keys, and security groups
- **Issue**: The statement applies account-wide with no ARN restriction.
- **Impact**: Access extends to every production database, every encryption key, and every network control, far beyond the application's functional need.
- **Least-Privilege Target**: Scope `ProdAppAccess` to:
  - `arn:aws:s3:::prod-app-data`
  - `arn:aws:s3:::prod-app-data/*`
  - `arn:aws:dynamodb:us-east-1:123456789012:table/prod-orders`
  - `arn:aws:logs:us-east-1:123456789012:log-group:/prod/app:*`
- **Correction Required**: The reviewer found that `Query` on `prod-orders` also requires `arn:aws:dynamodb:us-east-1:123456789012:table/prod-orders/index/*`. Add this ARN, or `Query` calls against secondary indexes will fail.

### Missing MFA/Conditions

❌ **CRITICAL**: No MFA requirement for create, delete, and modify actions in production.
- **Principal**: `AllowEverything`
- **Gap**: No `aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent` condition on destructive or modifying actions.
- **Impact**: Automated or stolen credentials can perform production changes and deletions without a second factor.
- **Required Correction**: The proposed MFA condition, if applied to the whole `ProdAppAccess` statement, would deny service principals (EC2 instance roles, Lambda execution roles, and ECS task roles), because they cannot satisfy `aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent`. Split the access into two statements:
  - An interactive statement for human principals that includes the `Bool` `aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent: true` and `NumericLessThan` `aws:MultiFactorAuthAge: 3600` conditions.
  - A workload statement with the same scoped actions and resources, without the MFA condition, attached only to the application's service roles.

❌ **HIGH**: No network or transport conditions.
- **Principal**: `AllowEverything`
- **Gap**: No `aws:SourceIp`, `aws:SecureTransport`, `aws:RequestedRegion`, or time-based restrictions.
- **Impact**: Credentials can be used from any network location, over unencrypted channels, in any region, at any time.
- **Recommendation**: Add `aws:SecureTransport: true` to all scoped statements. Add `aws:RequestedRegion` set to `us-east-1` to match the scoped resource ARNs. Add `aws:SourceIp` for interactive statements where corporate egress ranges are known.

### Session Credential Issues

❌ **LOW**: No session duration constraints.
- **Policy**: `AllowEverything`
- **Gap**: No `aws:TokenIssueTime` or `sts:DurationSeconds` limits.
- **Impact**: Credentials assuming this access may remain valid for the maximum session duration, extending the exposure window if they are leaked.
- **Recommendation**: Set `MaxSessionDuration` to `3600` on the role that carries the policy. Note that this is a role configuration setting, not a policy statement element. Confirm that long-running workloads can tolerate hourly credential refresh, or set a longer value for those roles only.

### Privilege Escalation Risks

⚠️ **Not rated by analyzer; flagged for verification by reviewer**: Indirect escalation paths may remain.
- **Path**: `sts:AssumeRole` and `iam:PassRole` through other attached or assumable policies
- **Issue**: The removed `*` grant included IAM and role-assumption actions. The proposed fix does not verify whether any other policy attached to the role, or any role it can assume, can recreate IAM, KMS, or delete access.
- **Impact**: If a reachable role carries administrator-level permissions, narrowing `AllowEverything` would reduce direct access without eliminating the effective privilege.
- **Mitigation**:
  1. Enumerate all policies attached to the affected role, including inline and permissions-boundary policies.
  2. Enumerate all roles reachable through `sts:AssumeRole` from this principal and review their policies.
  3. Confirm that no `iam:PassRole` grant pairs with a privileged service role.
  4. Apply a permissions boundary to the role that caps effective permissions at the `ProdAppAccess` scope.

### Verdict: ESCALATE

The fixes narrow an admin-grade production role, but the MFA condition would block non-interactive principals (EC2, Lambda, and ECS task roles cannot satisfy `aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent`), the DynamoDB ARN omits `table/prod-orders/index/*` needed for `Query`, and the proposal does not verify that other attached or assumable policies (for example, through `sts:AssumeRole` or `iam:PassRole`) cannot reproduce the removed IAM, KMS, and delete access. Production blast-radius and service-breakage decisions require human sign-off.

## Unified Recommendations

Recommendations are ordered by priority. Complete the CRITICAL items before implementing any change to the `AllowEverything` statement.

**CRITICAL**

1. **Verify escalation paths before narrowing.** Enumerate all policies attached to the affected role, all roles it can assume, and any `iam:PassRole` grants. Document which paths could reproduce IAM, KMS, or delete access. Owner: IAM/security team.
2. **Split the policy by principal type.** Create:
   - `ProdAppAccess-Interactive`: scoped actions, MFA condition (`aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent: true`, `aws:MultiFactorAuthAge < 3600`), attached to human roles.
   - `ProdAppAccess-Workload`: scoped actions, no MFA condition, attached to EC2, Lambda, and ECS task roles.
   
   Both statements must use the same ARN-scoped resources.
3. **Correct the DynamoDB resource list.** Include both `arn:aws:dynamodb:us-east-1:123456789012:table/prod-orders` and `arn:aws:dynamodb:us-east-1:123456789012:table/prod-orders/index/*`.
4. **Obtain human approval.** Record sign-off on blast radius and service impact before the change
